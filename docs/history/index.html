What the comparison shows

A modest overall gap hides larger annual differences.

These findings summarize the fixed 2013–2021 matched cohort across all three counties. They stay the same when you change the explorer filters. Each positive is a cell-year with at least one recorded discovery, not a count of individual fires.

The pooled estimate is above the recorded rate.

Across 8,588 eligible cell-years, 656 were recorded positive: an observed rate of 7.64%. The conservative model's mean probability was 8.34%, a gap of +0.70 percentage points. Summed probabilities imply 716.1 expected positive cell-years, compared with 656 observed.

Annual predictions vary less than observations.

The combined annual observed rate ranged from 4.73% to 12.81%; mean conservative probability ranged from 7.38% to 8.99%. Predictions were above observations in eight of nine years. The smaller pooled gap therefore does not mean the model captured each year's variation.

2020 has the largest combined annual gap.

In the 874-cell eligible cohort, 112 cells were recorded positive (12.81%), while summed conservative probabilities implied 74.4 expected positive cells (8.51% mean probability). The gap was −4.30 percentage points. The source transition and 122 unknown labels limit interpretation; this comparison cannot identify the cause of the gap.

County differences remain visible

Washington has the highest pooled observed rate and mean predicted probability. All three counties have a positive pooled gap, but individual years can show the opposite direction. These are descriptive summaries, not evidence that county differences are statistically significant.

What these findings support

The saved model provides a spatial probability comparison, with visible annual and county differences between its estimates and the recorded labels. Agreement in an aggregate rate does not establish calibration for individual cells, spatial discrimination, or future forecasting skill.

Both sides exclude 230 unknown-label and 155 flagged-zero cell-years. Missing labels are not zeros; the 2020 reporting transition cannot be separated from real fire variation here. Pooled rates weight eligible cell-years, rather than giving each year equal weight, and do not replace the frozen study's annual model-selection metrics. These years informed model choices and are not an independent final evaluation. No new model fits, predictions, or later-period observations were used.

Evidence: matched annual and county summaries. Rates are computed before rounding; pp means percentage points. Study cells include non-WUI land and unequal clipped areas, so these values are not statewide, per-area hazard, or property-loss estimates.

04 / Interpretation

Read the record with its limits.

Cells are the observation unit.

One cell can contain several discoveries but contributes only one positive in a year. These charts show occupied cell-years, not unique incident counts or the number of distinct cells across the whole period.

Coverage changes the comparison.

Unknown labels stay unknown. The 2020 source transition coincides with reporting differences; this evidence cannot separate reporting changes from real fire variation. A no-record label does not prove that no fire occurred.

Area affects opportunity.

Cells have unequal clipped areas. Counts and rates describe these footprints, including non-WUI land. They are not hazard per square kilometer, property-loss estimates, or daily fire danger.

Use matching comparison cohorts.

The historical panel retains known reviewed zeros, including flagged zeros. The ML comparison excludes unknown labels and frozen flagged zeros from both sides. Its rates can therefore differ from the historical panel. The expected count is positive cells, not incidents. Separate geographic fits and repeated years remain dependent; no significance or operational-readiness claim is made.
